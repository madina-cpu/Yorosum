Buyer primer

What separates good collagen from label decoration

Collagen types

The body makes 28 types of collagen, but five do most of the work. For a skin and beauty product, Type I is the one that matters.

TypeWhere in the bodyUsual source
ISkin, bone, tendon. About 90% of the body's collagen.Fish (marine), bovine

All nine products here use fish collagen, which is mostly Type I. Once collagen is hydrolysed into peptides, the type label matters less than two numbers you can verify: how many milligrams are in each stick and how small the peptides are.

What "Da" means

A dalton (Da) is the unit for molecular weight. One dalton is roughly the mass of a hydrogen atom. Collagen peptides are sold by their average molecular weight: the smaller the number, the shorter the protein fragments.

Gelatin~15,000–250,000 Da

Why lower Da matters

  • The gut absorbs dipeptides and tripeptides (two or three amino acids, roughly 150–400 Da) intact through a dedicated transporter called PepT1. Larger fragments must be broken down first.
  • Collagen-specific peptides such as Pro-Hyp (228 Da) have been measured in human blood after people drank collagen peptides, and lab studies suggest they signal skin cells (fibroblasts) to produce new collagen.
  • A 300 Da average means most of the dose is already at di/tripeptide size. A 2,000 Da average means most of it is not.
  • An average can hide a wide spread. Ask suppliers for the molecular weight distribution (for example, % below 500 Da by SEC-HPLC), not just the headline number.

Size and dose work together. A small peptide at 200 mg still delivers 200 mg. Human skin studies on collagen peptides have generally used 2.5–10 g per day, so 2,500 mg is our full-score benchmark for dose.

Why vitamin C belongs in the stick

Vitamin C does not help collagen peptides get absorbed. It does something more important: it is the required cofactor for the two enzymes (prolyl and lysyl hydroxylase) that lock new collagen fibres into their stable triple-helix shape. Without enough vitamin C the body cannot build sound collagen from the building blocks the stick provides. In Korea, vitamin C carries the recognised function "needed for the formation and maintenance of connective tissue."

Ingredients that look good but do little at these doses

  • Glutathione: oral studies on skin used 250–1,000 mg a day of the active compound. The products here deliver 25 mg, 2 mg and 0.2 mg.
  • Biotin: 30 µg equals the daily adequate intake, a basic requirement most diets already meet. There is no evidence extra biotin improves skin or hair in people who are not deficient.
  • Hyaluronic acid: oral studies generally used 120–240 mg a day. The products here contain 0.02–0.4 mg, several hundred times less.

None of these score points unless they reach a meaningful dose. A long ingredient list built from micro-doses is a sign of marketing-led formulation.

What to ask any supplier before you order

  1. Certificate of analysis for collagen content per stick, not per box.
  2. Molecular weight distribution by SEC-HPLC, including % of peptides below 500 Da and 1,000 Da.
  3. Fish species and country of origin of the collagen raw material (for example, tilapia or pangasius, Vietnam).
  4. Heavy metals and microbiology test results per batch (lead, cadmium, mercury, arsenic).
  5. Full ingredient list with percentages, including sweeteners, acidulants and flavours that fill the rest of the stick.
  6. Food category. All nine are 기타가공품 (general processed food), not 건강기능식품 (health functional food), so efficacy claims such as whitening or wrinkle improvement are not permitted in Korean marketing.
  7. Certifications required for your market: HACCP, Halal, ISO 22000, and export health certificates.
  8. MOQ, shelf life and private-label options, including minimum run for custom flavour or packaging.
